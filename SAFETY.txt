MavJOY 1.1.0
============

MavJOY turns a USB gamepad into an RC transmitter. It drives a real
aircraft. Read this before the first flight.


BEFORE POWERING THE MODULE

  * Fit the antenna first. Powering a 1 W transmitter without one can
    destroy its output stage.
  * Do not feed the module's XT30 from 3S or above.
  * Do all bench testing with the propellers OFF.


THE FAILSAFE IS ON THE AIRCRAFT, NOT HERE

  If channel data stops - gamepad unplugged, app closed, Esc pressed -
  MavJOY stops writing frames entirely. The module's UART watchdog then
  drops the RF link and your receiver falls into its own failsafe.

  That receiver failsafe is the real failsafe. Set it up in ArduPilot,
  Betaflight or INAV and test it on the bench, props off, before flying.


WHEN THE LINK COMES BACK

  Recovery does not hand control back on its own. Every switch channel
  (CH5-16) is frozen at the value the model last actually received, and
  stays frozen until you move that control. Anything you moved during the
  outage never reached the model, so letting it take effect the moment the
  link returns would pull the model out of the failsafe it is holding.

  CH1-4 are never frozen. Roll, pitch, throttle and yaw answer the sticks
  the instant the link is back.


THROTTLE

  Three modes, and one of them has a trap.

  trigger   An analog trigger. Released means idle. You hold it for the
            whole flight, but letting go always means idle.

  ramp      Ratcheting. Hold the up control to increase, the down control
            to decrease. It stays where you left it, like a real throttle
            stick.

  axis      A raw stick axis, and the one to be careful with. Most gamepad
            sticks are sprung and spring back to the middle of their
            travel - and the middle of the range is HALF THROTTLE. Let go
            and the throttle does not fall to idle, it jumps to 50%. Use
            this mode only with a control that stays where you put it, such
            as a proper throttle lever.

  Whichever mode you use, a link will not start unless the throttle reads
  0%, and the Cut button drops it to idle instantly.


ARMING

  CH5 is the arm channel. Always, and nothing else is. While it reads high
  MavJOY refuses to change module settings.

  The ARM banner shows what the AIRCRAFT reports, not what CH5 is doing.
  CRSF has no armed frame, so each firmware says it differently - pick
  yours with the Firmware selector on the status bar BEFORE connecting.

    ArduPilot  appends a star to the flight mode name while disarmed, and
               only with RC_OPTIONS bit 12 set (add 4096). Without that
               parameter the banner has nothing to read and stays grey.

    INAV       sends a real mode name only while armed, and OK, WAIT or
               !ERR while disarmed. Nothing to set up, and it is known
               from the first frame. "OK" on the ground is correct: it
               means disarmed with nothing wrong, not a fault.


FILES

  MavJOY.exe writes config.json beside itself. Keep them together. That
  file holds your port, mapping, output endpoints and the latch positions
  carried over from the last run.

  File > Export configuration writes the whole setup to a file you can
  copy to another machine and read back with File > Import. Latch
  positions are deliberately left out, so nothing comes up armed on the
  other machine because it was armed on this one.
